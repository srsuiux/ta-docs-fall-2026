Part 1: Convert to 1NF

1. Resulting Table:

Student_Orders
---------------------------------------
Student_Name (PK) | Order_Item (PK)
---------------------------------------
Todd Jones        | Bluebook
Todd Jones        | Pencils
Todd Jones        | Notebook
John Smith        | Pens
John Smith        | Notebook
John Smith        | Printer Paper
Mark Jones        | Bluebook
Mark Jones        | Pencils
Mark Jones        | Notebook

2. Primary Key:
- Composite Primary Key: (Student_Name, Order_Item)

3. Design Choices & Justification:
- In the original unnormalized table, the "Order" column had multiple items lumped together in a single cell as a comma-separated list. To satisfy 1NF, every cell must be atomic, meaning it can only hold a single value. I broke down each item into its own individual row so that every purchased item is recorded separately.
- Because a single student now appears across multiple rows for their different items, "Student_Name" by itself can no longer uniquely identify a row. Therefore, I expanded the primary key into a composite primary key consisting of both (Student_Name, Order_Item) to keep every row unique.


Part 2: Convert to 2NF

1. Decomposed Tables:

Table 1: Course_Fees
----------------------------
CourseCode (PK) | Fees
----------------------------
CSIS 3722       | 100
CSIS 4822       | 200
ENGL 1550       | 300
MATH 5600       | 400

- Primary Key: CourseCode
- Foreign Key: None

Table 2: Student_Enrollments
------------------------------------
Y_Number (PK) | CourseCode (PK, FK)
------------------------------------
Y001          | CSIS 3722
Y002          | CSIS 4822
Y001          | ENGL 1550
Y003          | MATH 5600
- Composite Primary Key: (Y_Number, CourseCode)
- Foreign Key: CourseCode (references CourseCode in Course_Fees)

2. Explanation & Justification:
- In the original table, the primary key was a composite key made up of (Y_Number, CourseCode). However, the "Fees" column depended only on "CourseCode", which is just a part of the primary key. This is a partial dependency and violates 2NF.
- To fix this, I split the table into two. "Course_Fees" handles the course and its fixed fee (where CourseCode is the primary key), and "Student_Enrollments" tracks which students are in which classes. This removes the partial dependency because all non-key attributes now fully depend on the entire primary key of their respective tables.


Part 3: Convert to 3NF / BCNF

1. Decomposed Tables:

Table 1: Event_Results
---------------------------------------------
Event (PK)      | Year (PK) | Winner (FK)
---------------------------------------------
WrestleMania    | 1988      | Undertaker
SummerSlam      | 2022      | Roman Reigns
Royal Rumble    | 1999      | Vince McMahon
Survivor Series | 2000      | Jeff Hardy
- Composite Primary Key: (Event, Year)
- Foreign Key: Winner (references Winner in Winner_Details)

Table 2: Winner_Details
----------------------------------
Winner (PK)     | DOB
----------------------------------
Undertaker      | 03/24/1965
Roman Reigns    | 05/25/1985
Vince McMahon   | 08/24/1945
Jeff Hardy      | 08/31/1977
- Primary Key: Winner
- Foreign Key: None

2. Explanation & Justification:
- In the original table, the composite primary key was (Event, Year), which determined the Winner, but the Winner also determined the DOB (Winner -> DOB). Since DOB depended on Winner (a non-key column), this created a transitive dependency.
- To bring this into BCNF, we must ensure that every determinant is a candidate key. I separated the winner's birthdate into its own table called "Winner_Details" where "Winner" is the primary key. The "Event_Results" table keeps track of the event, year, and winner. Now, all determinants are candidate keys, completely eliminating the transitive dependency.
