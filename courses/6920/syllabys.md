## Course Description

CSCI 6920 examines the relationship between **information systems, individuals, organizations, and society**.

The course provides a detailed study of the principles, methodologies, and issues involved in **designing, implementing, and administering information systems** as organizational resources within networked and data-driven environments.

Students explore how information systems support organizational strategy, decision-making, collaboration, innovation, and digital transformation.

---

## Course Goals

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

* Explain the role of information systems within individuals, organizations, and society.
* Analyze how information systems support organizational strategy.
* Understand modern information and communication technologies.
* Examine enterprise architecture and emerging technologies such as cloud computing and 5G.
* Understand the role of databases and data warehouses in information systems.
* Analyze enterprise systems including ERP, CRM, and SCM.
* Evaluate digital platforms including websites, social media, and mobile applications.
* Understand business intelligence, analytics, and artificial intelligence applications.
* Examine collaboration technologies and emerging digital environments.
* Understand knowledge management and e-learning systems.
* Analyze ethical, privacy, security, and societal issues surrounding information systems.
* Understand systems development methodologies such as Agile and Waterfall.
* Understand IS project management and strategic planning.
* Evaluate information systems from both technical and organizational perspectives.

## Learning Approach

The course connects **information systems theory with practical organizational and technological applications**.

Students should progressively move from understanding individual information-system concepts to analyzing how multiple technologies work together within modern organizations.

The learning progression is:

**Understand → Analyze → Apply → Evaluate → Present**

Students should be prepared to:

1. Understand the fundamental concept.
2. Connect the concept to organizational needs.
3. Examine real-world technology applications.
4. Analyze benefits, limitations, risks, and trade-offs.
5. Evaluate alternative approaches.
6. Communicate findings through written and presentation-based work.

# Major Course Themes

### 1. Information Systems & Organizations

Understanding how information systems influence organizational structure, decision-making, strategy, and operations.

### 2. Technology Infrastructure

Exploring enterprise architecture, cloud computing, networking technologies, 5G, and other emerging ICT technologies.

### 3. Data & Information Management

Examining databases, data warehouses, SQL, blockchain, business intelligence, and analytics.

### 4. Enterprise & Digital Systems

Understanding ERP, CRM, SCM, websites, social media, and mobile applications as components of modern information systems.

### 5. Artificial Intelligence & Emerging Technologies

Examining AI technologies, including systems such as ChatGPT, and their applications within business intelligence, analytics, and organizational decision-making.

### 6. People, Collaboration & Knowledge

Exploring collaboration technologies, knowledge management, e-learning, and emerging digital environments such as the metaverse.

### 7. Ethics, Privacy & Security

Analyzing the social and organizational implications of information systems, including privacy, security, ethics, and responsible technology use.

### 8. Systems Development & Management

Understanding software/system development methodologies, procurement, project management, and strategic planning.

# Graduate-Level Expectations

As a graduate-level course, students are expected to move beyond simply describing technologies.

Graduate-level work should demonstrate the ability to:

* **Analyze** information systems within organizational contexts.
* **Compare** competing technologies and methodologies.
* **Evaluate** technological decisions and their consequences.
* Identify assumptions, risks, limitations, and trade-offs.
* Connect theoretical concepts to real-world systems.
* Research contemporary information-system technologies.
* Form evidence-based arguments.
* Communicate technical and organizational findings clearly.
* Work collaboratively on complex information-system problems.
* Develop and defend recommendations.

A strong graduate-level response should answer not only:

> **"What is this technology?"**

but also:

> **"Why does it matter, where should it be used, what are its limitations, and what consequences does it create?"**

## Course Philosophy

> **Understand the system → connect it to the organization → analyze the technology → question its assumptions → evaluate its impact → communicate your findings.**

The objective of this course is not simply to learn about information systems. It is to develop the ability to **critically analyze how technology, data, people, organizations, and society interact within modern information systems**.
